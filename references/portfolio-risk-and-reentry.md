# Portfolio risk, trade purpose and re-entry

Use this module for holdings, staged purchases, stop-loss reviews, replacements and drawdown questions. Distill the presenter's process; never copy the presenter's historical prices or personal limits.

## Separate security merit from implementation

Give two judgments: (1) business quality, valuation and prospective return at the current price; (2) fit, timing and size in the user's actual portfolio. Keep an attractive overlapping candidate on the list. Signal concentration through issuer, sector, geography, customer, funding source and common economic driver. Explain an overlap's consequences before proposing a smaller tranche, slower staging, optional switch or cash funding. Do not invent universal concentration ceilings or let concentration alone veto a candidate. Apply a hard ceiling only if the user has adopted it.

Compare a replacement with keeping the incumbent, buying the candidate with available cash, and waiting. Use a common horizon, currency/FX assumptions and stress. Assess business resilience, valuation, catalyst timing, liquidity and shared dependencies. A previously profitable holding is not inherently safer to add to. Similar beta does not establish equal downside, liquidity or business risk; beta varies by sample and regime. Selling a loser does not oblige buying the same name back.

## Separate core and tactical positions

Declare trade purpose before choosing a trigger:

| Purpose | Main evidence | Exit or review logic |
|---|---|---|
| Core investment | Durable earnings/cash flow, balance sheet, valuation and horizon | Thesis change, return opportunity, or adopted portfolio risk limit |
| Tactical position or tranche | Observable range, breakout/retest, catalyst and reward/risk | Predefined technical failure, event outcome, target zone or expiry |
| Recovery/turnaround | Evidence of stabilization and a viable business/funding path | Failure to meet the specified recovery milestones |

Do not relabel a failed tactical trade as a long-term investment to avoid recognizing failure. A core holding can survive ordinary chart volatility, but “long term” does not override solvency, an adopted risk limit or an impaired thesis. A profitable tactical trim may coexist with a positive long-term view.

For rolling trades, retain a dated record of the original tranche, sale condition, proceeds, re-entry condition and failure/expiry rule. Compare the whole path, including dividends, cash yield, fees, applicable tax, slippage and missed upside, with holding over the same dates. Several small wins do not establish a repeatable annual return. State whether a profit figure concerns the trade, remaining shares or the whole portfolio; lowering displayed cost basis does not create economic return by itself.

## Budget losses before adding

Use current marked-to-market portfolio weights for prospective portfolio risk. Treat cost-based unrealized loss and peak-to-current drawdown as separate measures. A low purchase cost does not protect today's wealth from a future decline.

- For an unlevered holding with current weight `w` and an assumed forward price loss `d`, its first-order portfolio loss contribution is `w × d`. For multiple holdings under one coherent stress, sum their scenario contributions with relevant FX effects. Do not sum unrelated worst cases and present them as a probable portfolio outcome.
- To translate an adopted single-position loss budget `b` into an indicative maximum weight, use `w ≤ b/d`, with `d > 0`. This is conditional scenario arithmetic, not a guaranteed loss cap or a statistically calibrated VaR. It ignores interactions unless they are modeled separately.
- Example with synthetic inputs: an 8% position falling 25% from today contributes about 2% of current portfolio value in losses, before other positions and FX. The 25% is an example, not a rule.
- Distinguish planned stop loss from gap/liquidity stress. Close-confirmed stops may execute materially below the threshold. Daily monitoring cannot remove overnight gaps or make correlated positions independent.
- If current weights, cash or account constraints are missing, disclose the gap and provide conditional sizing rather than invented share counts. Do not hard-code old holdings or tax assumptions into this reusable skill.

The February source sequence uses a personal 25% position-loss limit and later discusses a personal 15% issuer ceiling. These are historical choices, not recommended defaults. Derive tolerable losses from the user's current constraints. If the user has configured a loss-only checker, apply that checker only when the position is in a loss on its defined basis; assess profit-taking, concentration and thesis changes separately rather than mislabelling them as stop-loss alerts.

## Stage entries and distinguish three exit reasons

For every tranche, state proposed size, maximum total commitment, funding/cash reserve, valuation condition, technical/event condition and cancellation rule. Distinguish a standing limit order from “review after a qualifying close”; reaching a price is insufficient when the plan also requires stabilization. Do not add merely because price or average cost is lower. Recalculate the thesis and total stress exposure after each fill.

1. **Technical invalidation:** the specific traded structure fails on the defined timeframe. State intraday versus close confirmation and executable next step.
2. **Portfolio risk limit:** exposure or scenario losses exceed an adopted budget, even if estimated value remains above price.
3. **Fundamental invalidation:** demand, moat, earnings quality, financing or another central assumption deteriorates.

Several can occur together. State which actually triggered. If a limit was breached, record it; do not silently widen it or use a revised valuation to erase the prior rule. A hard rule adopted by the user takes precedence over the presenter's historical preferences.

## After a failed thesis or stop

Preserve the dated original view and distinguish an analytical mistake, an acknowledged adverse scenario and an execution error. Do not use a later bounce to conclude that honoring a previously adopted risk limit was necessarily wrong.

Continue researching an exited security where relevant. Re-entry requires a fresh plan based on current price, post-event fundamentals and liquidity/technical evidence; it is not a mission to win back the loss. Compare re-entry, a better substitute and cash on the same forward basis. Explain what evidence cancels an old plan or justifies raising a previously unfilled entry level. If the recovery occurs without the required confirmation, acknowledge the missed opportunity rather than retrospectively claiming participation.

For suspected forced selling, test the actual seller, vehicle, asset and funding link. Private credit exposure, listed BDC shares, private fund redemptions and hedge-fund public-equity positions are distinct. A loan exposure to software companies does not prove ownership or forced liquidation of the corresponding listed shares. A technical rebound is evidence of price behavior, not proof that funding stress or an industry disruption has ended.
