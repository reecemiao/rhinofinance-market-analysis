# Market regime, transmission and positioning

## Market map

Use the relevant local market's benchmarks. For the source corpus's US review, compare S&P 500/SPY, Nasdaq-100/QQQ, Dow, Russell 2000/IWM, sector ETFs, semiconductors/SOXX or SMH, and software/IGV when relevant. Do not confuse Nasdaq Composite with Nasdaq-100, SOX with SOXX, or ETF prices with index levels.

Check equal-weight versus cap-weight performance, advancing/declining stocks, new highs/lows, constituent participation, and leading stocks' contribution. Broad weakness masked by a few mega-caps differs from broad improvement. Test a claimed software/semiconductor rotation using relative returns, constituents and available flows; inverse daily returns alone do not demonstrate a transfer of capital. Avoid treating changing historical correlations as fixed laws. Verify sector classifications and ETF composition/weighting: similar labels can hide different constituent concentration. Rank sectors by improving, stalled or broken structure when screening; verify the stock separately after an ETF breakout.

For a material macro release, compare actual, dated consensus, prior and revised prior. Identify the surprise and causal channel: growth/demand, margins/input costs, discount rates, funding conditions, FX or risk premium. Distinguish an inflation decline caused by supply normalization from one driven by demand destruction. Separate policy rates, expected policy path, inflation expectations, real yields and term premium. Rate cuts can coincide with growth stress; higher yields can reflect growth or fiscal/inflation stress.

Build a short chain: event → expectation revision → earnings/discount-rate/flow effect → sectors/stocks → evidence that would disprove it. State competing explanations when attribution is uncertain. Verify the release, speaker, timing and source; do not inherit historical transcript assertions about officeholders, events or policies. Check whether the news preceded the price move and whether relevant cross-assets corroborate the explanation. For policy projections, compare dispersion and changes as well as the median; a shift in communication framework can change the relevance of old indicators.

## Liquidity and balance sheets

Distinguish Treasury debt issuance, Treasury cash-management buybacks, Fed asset purchases, QT, TGA changes, reverse repos, bank reserves and private credit. Identify funding and balance-sheet entries before calling an operation liquidity injection or QE. A bond buyback funded by new issuance need not be net monetary stimulus. Explain why the relevant mechanism affects equities instead of assigning a universal liquidity signal.

For AI/capex cycles, link customer monetization and cash generation → capex and financing → supplier revenue, margin and backlog → earnings expectations and index concentration. Test circular financing, counterparty/customer concentration, funding maturity, debt-service burden, utilization and pricing. A capex growth slowdown is not necessarily a spending decline; a backlog/order announcement is not immediately recognized revenue or free cash flow.

## Flow evidence hierarchy

| Evidence | What it can show | Required limits |
|---|---|---|
| Fund flows or prime-broker survey | Reported buying/selling or positioning for that coverage universe | Provider, observation dates, lag, universe; not the whole market |
| Gross/net hedge-fund exposure | Leverage versus directional exposure | Define denominator and long/short convention; high gross is not high net bullishness |
| 13F | Reported quarter-end holdings and changes | Filing lag, incomplete instruments/shorts, options notional versus premium, position changes versus price changes; not live holdings |
| CTA or vol-control estimate | Model-implied buying/selling conditional on price/volatility paths | Dated model and horizon, upside/flat/downside cases, provider-specific trigger levels; no guaranteed orders |
| Options volume/OI/skew/gamma | Activity, option pricing and modeled positioning | Volume versus open interest, expiry/strike, opening/closing and customer/dealer sign often unknown; not a direct directional vote |
| Retail or leveraged-ETF activity | Participation/crowding within observed sources | Method, lag, leverage/reset effects, incomplete coverage; no universal retail behavior |
| Short interest/borrow data | Potential crowding and squeeze vulnerability | Settlement/report lag, float definition, borrow cost; an elevated level is not a timing signal |

Explain both branches of crowding: a reversal can force liquidation, while continued strength can trigger short covering or systematic buying. Do not label every rally a squeeze without supporting evidence. When a paid/proprietary series is unavailable, explicitly omit its numeric estimate and use public observations without pretending equivalence.

Flag expiry, index rebalancing, month/quarter-end, IPO allocations, holidays and shortened sessions. Assess whether these distort volume or returns before drawing a durable demand conclusion. A large IPO does not mechanically drain an equal amount from every other stock.

Distinguish turnover from executable market depth, spreads and price impact. A busy market can still be fragile. Distinguish gross purchases from net flow, increased shorts from reduced longs, and declining to add from selling. Keep CTA upside/flat/downside scenarios separate; do not sum mutually exclusive flows. Classify potential selling as already realized, currently occurring or conditional on an unmet trigger. Rising market capitalization is not an equal-dollar demand for fresh inflows, and cap-weighted funds' existing positions can appreciate without forced purchases merely to keep the new weights. Distinguish actual fund flows and mandate changes from this arithmetic.

VIX concerns option-implied S&P 500 volatility, not a direct probability of decline. Low index volatility can coexist with high single-stock volatility when correlations are low; establish implied correlation with the proper option/weight data rather than equating it to a visual divergence. Do not make a historical VIX threshold a universal sell signal.

For bubble/cycle questions, compare evidence on valuation, earnings expectations, concentration, issuance, positioning/leverage, volatility and realized fundamentals; show both extreme and non-extreme readings. Separate investment share of GDP, change in that share, growth rate and contribution to GDP growth. Historical resemblance suggests hypotheses, not a crisis date or causal proof. State each proxy's observation window, sample coverage and alternative explanations.

For 13F interpretation, refresh the [SEC's Form 13F FAQ](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/staff-guidance/division-investment-management-frequently-asked-questions/frequently-asked-questions-about-form-13f), especially questions 25 and 41–44. For reported Treasury transactions, use [TreasuryDirect buyback announcements and results](https://www.treasurydirect.gov/auctions/announcements-data-results/buy-backs/) and distinguish announced capacity from accepted purchases.
