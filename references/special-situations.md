# Conditional modules

Use only the section needed for the question. Options-market evidence does not require recommending an options trade.

## Option-implied event ranges and hedges

Identify timestamp, underlying price, expiry, strike/moneyness and the method behind an expected move. An ATM-straddle cost and an annualized-IV × square-root-time estimate are different conventions; neither automatically gives a real-world 68% interval. Earnings jumps, skew and fat tails weaken a normal-distribution shortcut. An outcome outside a one-standard-deviation estimate is not automatically a black swan. Overlay a sourced implied range on support/resistance to ask whether a plausible event move crosses a regime boundary, without calling that range a floor or ceiling.

Distinguish option volume, OI, premium, underlying notional, customer/dealer exposure and opening/closing activity. A standard 100-share contract multiplier is not 100× leverage. Confirm actual multiplier and exercise style for the instrument, especially adjusted contracts. OI alone does not reveal dealer gamma sign; dealer hedging explanations require a sourced model and its assumptions. A slow favorable stock move can still lose on short-dated long options through time decay or an IV fall.

If explicitly asked for protection or permitted derivative strategies, show expiration payoff and cost, coverage shares, liquidity, expiry, exercise/assignment and residual risks. For a purchased put spread with strikes Kh > Kl and net debit d, maximum payoff is Kh−Kl and maximum option profit is Kh−Kl−d per share; below Kl its additional protection stops, so an underlying stock can keep losing. A collar trades premium cost and downside protection against a capped upside; identify the short-call obligation. Do not present either as free or complete protection. Use current option specifications before implementation.

## IPOs, lockups and equity supply

Separate issuer capital raised, secondary shares sold, total market capitalization and tradable float. Distinguish offer price from the first exchange trade and specify the baseline for a first-day return. Turnover can include repeated trades in the same shares. Low float increases price sensitivity in both directions; it does not guarantee a squeeze or appreciation.

Use a dated event calendar for allocation, listing, stabilization/greenshoe terms, options eligibility, index inclusion and staged lockup expiration. Verify prospectus and exchange/index rules; eligibility or a proposal is not completed inclusion. Unlock eligibility is not actual insider selling. Compare price/volume absorption with actual supply disclosures. An analyst upgrade does not itself compel passive-index buying.

For net equity supply, distinguish executed repurchases and net issuance from announced authorizations, stock compensation, secondary turnover, unlocks and market-value changes. A repurchase authorization is not an executed share reduction; a 7% authorization-to-market-cap ratio does not imply a 7% stock return. Check funding, price paid, dilution and foregone uses of cash. Management buying does not establish intrinsic value or a floor.

## Vendor financing, contracts and credit

Map customer, financier, guarantor and supplier separately. Distinguish equity investments, loans, leases, guarantees and milestone-linked warrants. Estimate revenue benefits, dilution, cash commitments, recourse, contingent loss, customer concentration and alternative buyers. Show whose balance sheet bears failure; a canceled fringe project is not automatically a broken industry funding chain.

Read material contract terms before treating backlog as cash protection: duration, price floors, minimum volume/take-or-pay, milestones, cancellation, renegotiation and customer creditworthiness. An embedded-option analogy can explain economics but does not substitute for the contract or imply an exchange-traded hedge.

Keep CDS spread level, spread change, maturity and quoted units separate from default probabilities. A simple constant-hazard approximation uses h≈spread/(1−recovery) and cumulative risk-neutral PD≈1−exp(−hT), with spread as a decimal per year. Label assumptions and liquidity/risk-premium effects; do not report the result as an objective real-world default probability or infer it from an equity price decline. Use a full calibrated curve when precision matters, and verify the methodology before using it.
